Budgeting

Budgeting is the process of planning how income will be allocated across expenses, savings, and financial goals. A useful budget compares expected income with essential and discretionary expenses. Tracking spending by category can help identify areas where unnecessary spending may be reduced.


Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected expenses such as medical costs, urgent repairs, or temporary loss of income. An emergency fund should generally be kept in an accessible and relatively low-risk form. The appropriate amount depends on a person's income, expenses, employment stability, and financial responsibilities.


Savings

Savings represent the portion of income that is not spent. Building regular savings can provide financial security and help fund future goals. A consistent savings habit is generally more important than saving a large amount only occasionally.


Inflation

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services over time. When inflation increases, the purchasing power of money decreases, meaning the same amount of money may buy fewer goods and services.


Financial Health

Financial health describes the overall condition of a person's finances. Important factors can include income stability, spending levels, savings, debt obligations, emergency reserves, and the ability to meet financial goals.


Expense Management

Expense management involves monitoring and controlling spending. Reviewing spending by category can help identify high-cost areas. Essential expenses should generally be distinguished from discretionary expenses before deciding where reductions can be made.


Investing

Investing involves putting money into assets with the expectation of achieving future financial growth or income. Investment decisions should consider financial goals, investment time horizon, liquidity requirements, and tolerance for risk. Past performance does not guarantee future returns.


Diversification

Diversification means spreading investments across different assets or investment categories rather than concentrating money in a single investment. Diversification can help reduce the impact of poor performance from one particular investment, although it cannot eliminate investment risk.


Fixed Deposits

A fixed deposit is a financial product in which money is deposited with a bank or financial institution for a specified period, generally at a predetermined interest rate. The suitability of a fixed deposit depends on factors such as liquidity needs, interest rates, and financial goals.


Mutual Funds

A mutual fund pools money from multiple investors and invests it according to a defined investment strategy. Different mutual funds have different levels of risk, objectives, fees, and asset allocations. Investors should review the fund's characteristics before investing.


Debt Management

Debt management involves understanding outstanding debts, interest rates, repayment schedules, and the effect of debt payments on cash flow. Prioritising high-cost debt and maintaining timely repayments can help improve financial stability.


Financial Goals

Financial goals are specific objectives involving money, such as building an emergency fund, purchasing a home, funding education, or planning for retirement. Goals are easier to manage when they have a defined amount, time frame, and savings plan.


House Purchase Planning

Planning for a house purchase may involve considering the property price, down payment, loan amount, interest rate, loan tenure, existing debt, income stability, emergency savings, and other household expenses. Expense data alone cannot determine whether a person will qualify for a home loan.


Affordability

Affordability means whether a purchase can reasonably be supported by a person's available financial resources without creating excessive financial pressure. A purchase should be considered alongside income, regular expenses, existing savings, emergency reserves, and other financial obligations.
